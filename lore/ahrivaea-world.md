# The World of Ahrivaea

_World reference provided by jeffreystu10. Everything in the mod should stay consistent with this document._

Welcome to Ahrivaea!
The World of Ahrivaea (Ah-riv-AY-uh) is a vast and living world, rich with tales of heroism, peril, discovery, and forgotten legend. From the sun-scorched lowland deserts where dunes roll like golden seas, to the ice-shrouded peaks of towering mountain ranges that pierce the heavens, adventure stirs in every corner of the realm.

Ahrivaea is shaped by two great continents that stand in watchful opposition across narrow waters: Saborah (suh-BORE-uh) and Faestrum (FAY-strum). Their names are drawn from the oldest creation myth — a story of division, destiny, and divine design — and though they are separate lands, their peoples have long crossed the seas between them. Trade, war, pilgrimage, and ambition ensure that neither continent exists in isolation.

The Azure Horizon is a narrow ocean that winds like a blade-cut between Saborah and Faestrum. Though slender in breadth, its waters run deep and restless, scattered with countless islands said to be fragments of the world’s first breaking. Sailors claim that on still nights, the tides whisper of the age when the continents were one.

Beneath all lies Thelanterrae (thel-AN-terh-ay) — the Realm Below — a vast and winding labyrinth of tunnels, chasms, and lightless caverns that stretch beneath the entire world. There, in the stone-veined deep, thrive ancient cultures and hidden kingdoms collectively known to surface dwellers as the Underlings. Their cities glow with strange fires, and their histories are carved not in ink, but in rock.

Above the depths stands Ahrivaea proper — the realm of soil and storm, of mountains, deserts, forests, and open sea — where most of the world’s peoples wage their wars, build their empires, and chase their legends.

And higher still, beyond the reach of ordinary flight, drifts Caeloryn (kay-LORE-in)  — a realm of cloud-seas and floating sky-isles suspended in endless blue. There, nations cling to stone suspended in air, and cultures shaped by wind and horizon flourish among the heavens, as if the sky itself had learned to breathe.

Across both lands and the surrounding water dwell countless races and cultures — ancient, magical, and fiercely distinct. Each leaves its mark upon the world’s unfolding story, shaping its history through triumph, tragedy, and the relentless pursuit of legacy.

In Ahrivaea, the age of legend is not past. It waits just beyond the horizon.

The Current Update - 
In Ahrivaea, the current year is 312 of the Second Age of Founding (312.2AF).

The Treaty of the Four Corners was signed on Highday, 1st Cycle of First Harvest, Year 0 A.F. For more than three centuries, the four most powerful kingdoms — collectively known as the Four Corners — have stood in wary alliance. Born from the ashes of the previous age’s devastation, their pact was forged to reclaim the fractured realm and impose a lasting peace upon it. Graecia, A’sasera, Scrael Karnus, and The Sun Empire endure as the great pillars of that accord — uneasy partners bound not by trust, but by necessity.

Though they agree on little, the ancient treaty between them has never been broken. Its endurance has shaped an era of stability — some call it peace, others call it containment.

Yet unity has not gone unchallenged.

As the Four Corners strengthened their alliance, so too did the number and coordination of those known as Dreadcasters — wielders of the forbidden force called Anathema. What began as scattered insurgency has coalesced into something far more deliberate. From the shadows emerged the Dread Circle, a coven and warband of Dreadcasters who now wage selective but calculated war against the Four Corners and their allies.

They preach of an ending long foretold — of the unraveling of the Age of Founding and the ascension of a new and nameless divinity: the rumored God of Dread.

For years, such prophecies were dismissed as the ramblings of zealots. But as calamity spreads and fear takes root across the realm, fewer dare to laugh. Confidence in the Four Corners is crumbling. Uncertainty hides in every shadow, and distrust lingers in the air. 

Direct Conflicts For Your Games- 
In Ahrivaea, the story is yours to shape.

No chronicle is fixed, no outcome inevitable. You are not bound to established canon nor confined to a single path. The realm is vast, and within it lie countless threads of conflict, mystery, and ambition waiting to be drawn into your own tale.

For those who seek established tensions, the Dread Circle stands as the greatest organized threat to the Age of Founding — an open wound in the realm’s fragile peace. Yet they are far from alone.

From the shadows operates the Pale Brand, an intricate network of assassins, infiltrators, and political manipulators who believe true stability can only come through quiet domination. Their war is not waged on battlefields, but in council chambers and midnight corridors.

And deeper still, from the lightless reaches of Thelanterrae, ancient and primordial beings have begun to stir. Long-buried horrors now claw their way toward the surface, shaking cities and scattering settlements as they rise.

Yet even these forces are only foundations.

Perhaps one of the Four Corners grows weary of the ancient treaty and seeks to shatter it. Perhaps old rivalries reignite and civil war fractures a kingdom from within. Perhaps the God of Dread is only one of many rising powers — or perhaps the prophecy is a lie entirely.

Canon is not a cage. It is a compass.

In Ahrivaea, you may honor the established age… or be the one who ends it.

The Dread Circle
The Dread Circle teaches that the mortal soul is corrupted not by hate… but by dependence on love.
Each of the Seven Schools selects one of the so-called Mortal Loves and eradicates it completely from their being. Not temperance. Not discipline.

Erasure.They carve it out through ritual oath to Anathema, binding themselves to survive without the need for that part of their humanity. And in doing so, they become something else. They call it Purity. The rest of the realm calls it monstrosity.

Their bodies endure.
Their minds fracture.
Their humanity rots.

What sustains them is no longer food, ambition, desire, or identity.

It is Dread.

The Seven Schools of Severance
Each School sacrifices its chosen Mortal Love so completely that the absence becomes a deformity of soul and flesh.

The Starved — Gluttony Severed
They never eat. Not a crumb. Not a drop. Through oathbound Anathema, their bodies no longer require sustenance. Flesh withers but does not die. Veins darken. Skin clings to bone. Their eyes sink deep into skulls that should be long dead. But they feel hunger.

Endlessly.

The absence of indulgence does not bring peace — it brings obsession. They dream of feasts they cannot touch. They inhale the scent of food like addicts denied forever. Their magic manifests as draining force. They do not consume matter. They consume vitality.

The Devout — Sloth Severed
They do not rest. Ever. They have cut away the desire for stillness, comfort, or reprieve. They do not sleep. They do not pause. They do not slow. Their minds burn constantly. At first, this makes them terrifyingly efficient. Tireless scholars. Relentless hunters. Obsessive architects of ritual.

But without rest, the mind fractures. Hallucinations bloom. Thoughts loop. Paranoia festers. They become frantic vessels of compulsive motion — pacing, muttering, carving sigils into stone at all hours. Their Dread is exhaustion without escape.

The Quiet — Wrath Severed
They have removed anger. They do not rage. They do not shout. They do not flare. They cannot feel wrath. And so they kill without it. Their violence is cold, detached, surgical. No fury. No passion. Only decision. But the suppression of wrath does not eliminate harm — it removes the humanity that gives it context. They become disturbingly serene in atrocity. Their Dread is calm cruelty.

The Hollow — Envy Severed
They do not desire what others have. No jealousy. No comparison. No longing for another’s life. So they discard their own. Without envy to anchor self-definition, identity erodes. They mimic others not from jealousy — but from emptiness. Faces are copied. Voices borrowed. Personalities overwritten. They do not want what you have. They want to understand what being “someone” feels like. Their Dread is the absence of self.

The Sworn — Lust Severed
They sever desire. Not merely carnal longing — but yearning itself. No romance. No craving for closeness. No passion. They form bonds only through oath. Contracts. Vows. Pacts. Relationships are transactional and ritualized. Without desire, connection becomes a hollow ceremony. They simulate devotion but feel none. Their Dread is intimacy without warmth.

The Empty Palm — Greed Severed
They own nothing. They claim no titles, no land, no wealth. They refuse possessions. They reject accumulation. But in severing greed, they also sever attachment. They care for nothing. Not cities. Not allies. Not legacy. And so they manipulate resources without conscience. Wealth flows through them like water through bone. Their Dread is detachment from consequence.

The Nothing — Pride Severed
They have removed self-importance. No ego. No sense of specialness. No belief that they matter. And without pride, the boundary between self and oblivion thins.They seek erasure — of history, of monuments, of names. They speak softly of entropy as mercy. Their Dread is cosmic insignificance.

Some believe the prophecy of the God of Dread began with them — because only those who no longer believe they matter would dare usher in something greater than all existence.

The Cost of Severance
The Circle teaches that by sacrificing away these loves, they approach divine clarity. But what they truly create is imbalance.

Humanity is not meant to function, missing one of its primal drives. The void left behind festers. Warps. Amplified through Anathema.They are not free of love. They are defined by its absence. And that absence rots them.

Why They Unite
The Seven Schools distrust one another. There is a great deal of infighting and internal conflict between the Schools. Each believes its Severance is the purest path. But all share and are willing to unify under one truth:

To end the Age of Founding, they must force the realm into collective severance.The coming of the God of Dread, they believe, will strip the world of its illusions — just as they stripped themselves.



The Pale Brand 
The Pale Brand is not a cult. It is a correction.

Where the Four Corners call their unity “peace,” the Pale Brand calls it stagnation. Three centuries of treaty have calcified power into dynasties, insulated rulers from consequence, and replaced strength with bureaucracy.

The Brand believes stability breeds decay. So they destabilize — deliberately, precisely, and quietly. They are spies, blackmailers, political architects, poisoners, and professional ruiners of reputations. They do not seek to burn the world. They seek to own it.

Origin
The Pale Brand was born during the early years of the Age of Founding. When the Four Corners signed their treaty, thousands of ambitious generals, displaced nobles, intelligence agents, and war profiteers found themselves irrelevant overnight. Some adapted. Some resented. A small handful organized.

What began as a network of information traders became something far more intentional: a shadow infrastructure designed to influence the peace from within. They do not want chaos. They want leverage.

The Meaning of the Name
“Pale” refers to a boundary — a limit of control. “Brand” refers to a mark burned into something to claim ownership. Together, the name carries a quiet threat:

Everything has a boundary.
Everything can be marked.

Some initiates bear a faint, pale scar at the inside of the wrist — a stylized sigil burned in with alchemical ash. Others never mark themselves at all. Visibility is optional. Influence is not.

Structure
The Pale Brand is cellular. No grand temples. No centralized throne.

Instead:

Circles – Small operational cells within a city or region.

Grand Auditor - The unseen head of the Pale Brand. Sets long-term strategy and determines which powers are preserved, weakened, or removed.

Inner Ledger - A small ruling council overseeing major domains of influence (crowns, coin, steel, intelligence). They coordinate large-scale operations across the realm.

High Weavers - Regional directors managing intelligence networks across provinces or major cities.

Weavers - Mid-level coordinators who control local cells and assign missions. Most members report only to their Weaver.

Circlemasters - Leaders of individual city-based cells (“Circles”). Handle recruitment, secrecy, and localized influence.

Knives - Assassins and enforcers used for precise eliminations and intimidation.

Murmurs - Infiltrators embedded in courts, guilds, and institutions to gather secrets and shape decisions.
Quills - Specialists in forgery, propaganda, and misinformation.

Factors - Financial manipulators who influence trade, debt, and economic stability.

Collectors - Field agents who secure blackmail material and leverage.

Initiates - Recruits used for low-risk tasks until proven trustworthy.

The Ledger – A semi-mythical record of debts, secrets, and leverage points said to be maintained by the highest unseen authority.

Most members know only their Circle and their Weaver. Very few know the full scale of the organization.

Methods
The Pale Brand rarely wages open war.

Their tools are quieter:

Blackmail secured through surveillance or seduction

Financial destabilization of merchant houses

Carefully staged scandals

Poisonings disguised as illness

Inciting localized unrest to weaken rivals

Sponsoring coups through deniable intermediaries

If a kingdom falls into civil war, the Brand is likely funding both sides. If a treaty collapses, they probably wrote the speech that fractured it.

Philosophy
The Brand believes power should belong to those capable of wielding it efficiently. They see the Four Corners as bloated relics. Their ultimate aim is not simple domination, but replacement — installing rulers beholden to them, or manipulating existing ones until they function as extensions of the Brand’s will. They prefer:

Controlled instability.
Predictable corruption.
Dependence.
Peace is acceptable.
So long as they hold the strings.

Internal Tensions
The Pale Brand is not purely villainous. Some factions within it believe they are the only force preventing greater catastrophe. They argue:

“If we did not steer the realm, the Dread Circle would.”

Others simply crave control. And some want to end the Four Corners entirely — breaking the ancient treaty and reshaping the political map. There are rumors that some of the Four Corners may already be partially infiltrated.

The World Itself

The Ahrivaean Calendar
The Fivefold Cycle and the Omensdays
Time in Ahrivaea is measured not in seven-day weeks, but in five-day Cycles. Each Cycle moves with steady rhythm, shaping trade, worship, governance, and daily life across the realm.

The days of the Cycle are:

Lowday


Workday


Middleday


Highday


Restday


This fivefold pattern governs most of the year — predictable, steady, and dependable. But twice each year, the Cycle falters. And the world pauses.

The Five Days of the Cycle
Lowday: The beginning of the Cycle. Associated with preparation and foundation. Labor begins, contracts are drafted, and travel often sets out at dawn. It is considered a practical and grounded day.

Workday: The busiest day of trade and industry. Markets swell, guild halls convene, and most business is conducted. If something must be done efficiently, it is done on Workday.

Middleday: The central turning point of the Cycle. Courts hold hearings, proclamations are issued, and caravans commonly depart. It is regarded as a balanced day for decisions.

Highday: A day of gathering and observance. Festivals, sermons, civic ceremonies, and public celebrations are often held on Highday. It carries a sense of openness and visibility.

Restday: A quieter close to the Cycle. Families gather, shops close early, and accounts are settled. Beginning conflict or signing binding contracts on Restday is widely considered ill-omened.

After Restday, the Cycle begins again with Lowday.

Except twice each year.

Omensday
The Liminal Peaks
At the height of Summer and the depth of Winter, an extra day emerges between Restday and Lowday.

This day is known as Omensday.

It is not counted as part of any Cycle. Ledgers skip it. Contracts rarely acknowledge it. It stands outside the natural rhythm of time.

Omensday occurs only twice annually:


Highsummer Omensday – at the sun’s highest peak


Deepwinter Omensday – at the coldest, longest night


On these days, something changes.

The Nature of Omensday
Across Ahrivaea, it is widely accepted — even among skeptics — that Mithrile, the living current of magic, is stronger on Omensday.

Spells burn brighter.
Enchantments hum with greater resonance.
Rituals require less effort — and sometimes yield more than intended.
Nearly double the number of stars appear in the sky.

Many claim the Ethereal Realms drift closer to the mortal plane during Omensday. Thelanterrae feels nearer beneath the earth. The skies seem heavier above. Dreams grow vivid. The air carries a faint metallic sharpness.

To some, this is sacred convergence.

To others, it is superstition.

To most, it is simply a day that feels… different.

Highsummer Omensday
Celebrated openly and vibrantly. Lanterns hang from rooftops. Public magical exhibitions draw crowds. Oaths sworn on Highsummer Omensday are believed to bind more tightly than any other day of the year. It is considered auspicious for:

Beginning apprenticeships


Forging enchanted items


Swearing sacred vows


Mithrile flows warm and radiant, like sunlight through glass.

Deepwinter Omensday
Observed with caution and reverence. Candles burn through the longest night. Families gather indoors. Temples hold silent vigils. Divinations performed on Deepwinter Omensday are believed to reveal uncomfortable truths. Travel is rare. Sealed doors are common. Some say the veil feels thinnest during the deep hours before dawn. Mithrile moves slower, heavier, deeper. 

A Day Outside Time
The Four Corners suspend major decrees on Omensday. The Pale Brand sharpens its attention. The Dread Circle performs its most sacred Severance rituals. Yet for all its weight, Omensday is not universally feared. Children play in the streets during Highsummer. Snow lanterns glow during Deepwinter. Musicians perform. Food is shared. Stories are told. It is not a day of doom.

It is a reminder that the world of Ahrivaea is layered — and that, twice each year, those layers draw just a little closer together. And then, when Omensday passes, the Cycle begins again. Lowday returns. As if nothing happened at all.


Omensday - The Days Outside the Cycle
Twice each year, the five-day Cycle pauses. After Restday, instead of returning to Lowday, a sixth day manifests:

Omensday.

Omensday is not counted within any Cycle or Phase.

It occurs:

During Suncrest — Highsummer Omensday


During Frostfall — Deepwinter Omensday


On Omensday, Mithrile is stronger. Spells burn brighter. Rituals require less effort. Enchantments hum audibly to those sensitive to magic. Many believe the Ethereal Realms drift closer to the mortal world.

Highsummer Omensday is celebrated with lanterns, magical displays, and oath-swearing. It is rumored that those born on Highsummer Omensday are destined to feel more joy in life than everyone else.

Deepwinter Omensday is observed more quietly, with candles, vigils, and careful travel. It is rumored that those buried on Deepwinter Omensday are reborn again shortly after. 

To some, Omensday is sacred.
To others, it is superstition.
To most, it is simply a day that feels different.

When Omensday passes, the cycle resumes.

Lowday returns.

The Founding Age Calendar
Time in Ahrivaea
The Founding Age Calendar was established at the signing of the Treaty of the Four Corners, marking the beginning of the Age of Founding. It was created as a shared system of timekeeping to symbolize unity and stability across the realm.

Years are recorded as:

Year ___ of the Age of Founding (A.F.)

The current year is 312 A.F.

Structure of the Year
An Ahrivaean year consists of:

4 Seasons


6 Phases


72 Cycles


5 Days per Cycle


2 Omensdays


Each Cycle contains five days:

Lowday – Preparation and foundation


Workday – Focused on Trade and industry


Middleday – Decisions and decrees


Highday – Gatherings and ceremony


Restday – Reflection and settlement


Each year contains 72 Cycles (360 days), divided evenly into six Phases of 12 Cycles each.

Twice each year, an additional day — Omensday — occurs outside the normal Cycle structure.

The Six Phases of the Year
Each Phase contains 12 Cycles (60 days).

First Harvest
The early yield of Dremael. Markets swell and contracts are made. A season of prosperity and forward planning.

Second Harvest
The final gathering before winter. Storehouses are sealed and political tensions rise as regions measure their strength.

Bitterwind
The first true bite of Frijeal. Storms roll in, roads close, and endurance becomes paramount.

Frostfall
The deepest cold of Frijeal. Deepwinter Omensday occurs during Frostfall. Nights are longest and Mithrile feels heavy and resonant.

Rainrise
The thaw of Virelan. Rivers swell, planting begins, and trade routes reopen.

Suncrest
The height of Solmara. Highsummer Omensday occurs during Suncrest. Festivals flourish and Mithrile burns bright and energetic.

The Four Seasons
Each Season spans 18 Cycles and overlaps with the Phases:

Dremael (dreh-MY-all)  – First Harvest & Second Harvest


Frijeal (frih-HAY-all)  – Bitterwind & Frostfall


Virelan (vir-eh-LAN) – Rainrise


Solmara (sol-MAR-uh) – Suncrest

Mithrile
The Living Current of Ahrivaea
Mithrile is the foundational magical force of Ahrivaea. It is not chaotic, nor is it freeform — it is structured, intentional, and bound by natural law.

Every living being carries within them a fragment of Mithrile known as mana — a literal reservoir of magical energy bound to the soul. Even those who never study the Mithrile Arts possess it.

To cast a spell is to sacrifice mana, shaping Mithrile into form through structured practice. Mana can be exhausted, but not destroyed. With rest and time, it replenishes.

Magic in Ahrivaea is never improvised chaos.
It is organized through spells — deliberate, practiced forms that protect mortals from drawing more power than they can survive.

The Three Schools of Mithrile
All magical practice falls into one of three Schools:

Primordial

Arcane

Anathema

These are not philosophies. They are fundamentally different relationships to Mithrile itself.

Primordial Mithrile
Primordial Mithrile is the magic of creation, elements, life, death, and raw chaos. It is vast, untamed, and devastating.

It is the power of storm and flame.
Of stone and sea.
Of birth and annihilation.

Primordial Mithrile is considered the most powerful form of magic in existence.

Stonehearts
Only individuals known as Stonehearts can wield Primordial Mithrile directly.

They require no medium.

They summon Mithrile from within themselves.

They are extraordinarily rare (perhaps 1 in 1000).

Their mana reserves appear far greater than those of ordinary mortals.

No one knows why Stonehearts exist.

Some believe the gods created them to combat Dreadcasters.
Others believe they are accidents of creation — or something far older.

The truth remains unknown.

Arcane Mithrile
Arcane Mithrile is structured, deliberate magic.

It governs alteration, influence, enchantment, and warding. It is subtle compared to Primordial power — but far more common.

Arcane Mithrile was, according to many traditions, gifted to mortals by Equiaeus (EH-quee-ay-us)  to maintain balance in the world. Mortals were not entrusted with the raw force of creation — but they were given tools to defend themselves.

Practicing Arcane Mithrile
Anyone may study Arcane Mithrile, but it requires intense discipline.

It must be channeled through:

Runecraft (drawn sigils)


Mithrilespeak (verbal incantation in a lost magical tongue)


Arcane practitioners are commonly known as:

Wizards


Runesayers


Mystics


They cannot summon magic without their medium. Runes are drawn on paper, skin, or objects. Mithrilespeak requires memorization of complex ancient phrases.

Magic remains structured — always cast in spells.

Stone Holders
Occasionally, fragments of fallen stars descend from the heavens — glowing gemstones containing a single Primordial spell.

These are known as Starstones.

Those who possess them are called Stone Holders.

A stone allows only one specific spell.

It can be used only a limited number of times.

Its power fades with each use.

The wielder is not a true mage — merely a bearer of stored magic.

Stonehearts distrust Stone Holders deeply.

No one knows where the stones originate.

Anathema
Anathema is the corrupted form of Mithrile.

It is not a separate energy. It is Mithrile that has died — twisted into a force known as Dread.

Anathema is universally regarded as wicked.

Not all evil is Anathema.
But all Anathema is evil.

Nearly every religion, culture, and kingdom agrees on this point.

The Cost of Anathema
To practice Anathema requires:

Mana


A dialect of Mithrilespeak known as Dreadtongue


The genuine sacrifice of someone or something the caster loves, most commonly themselves. 


The sacrifice must be real. If it is not genuine, the magic fails.

The toll is irreversible.

Anathema scars the physical, emotional, and spiritual self.

Those who wield it are known as Dreadcasters.

The Origin of Anathema
Many traditions claim Anathema was created centuries ago when mortals, aided by certain deities, attempted to wield Primordial forces beyond their design.

Mithrile resisted.

It fractured.

In doing so, it birthed Dread.

This account varies by culture. No confirmed texts prove it.

There is widespread rumor of a God of Dread, though no historical evidence has ever been found. Some scholars argue the concept exists simply because mortals seek to personify power.

Mana
Mana is the personal reservoir of Mithrile within each soul.

It fuels all magic.

It grows stronger through practice

It can be exhausted but not destroyed.

Rest restores it.

Stonehearts possess unusually deep wells of mana.

Arcane practitioners draw from their own pools.

Dreadcasters require mana in addition to sacrifice.

Magic is not limitless.

It is measured, structured, and costly.

Summary of Power Hierarchy
Primordial – Rare, elemental, devastating
Arcane – Structured, learned, defensive
Anathema – Corrupted, sacrificial, damning

Cultural Consensus
Primordial is feared but not condemned.

Arcane is difficult but accepted.

Anathema is universally considered evil.

Not all evil is Anathema.
But all Anathema is evil.

The Truth of Ahrivaea
The Archaeon and the Order of the Divine

The Nature of the Gods
The gods of Ahrivaea are cosmic beings.

They do not possess physical forms within the mortal realm. Were they to reveal their true nature, it is believed the sight would drive mortals mad — or kill them outright.

They are not good.
They are not evil.

They exist in perfect alignment with their domains.

To mortals, their actions may appear benevolent or cruel, but such judgments are born of limited perspective. The gods exist beyond mortal morality.

Avatars
Though the gods cannot manifest directly, they may inhabit an Avatar — a mortal vessel through which they interact with the world.

Such events are extraordinarily rare. A god may assume an Avatar perhaps once a century — sometimes less.

Avatars are not chosen lightly.
Their existence often reshapes history.

The Archaeon
The collective council of all true gods is known as The Archaeon.

This is not a parliament, nor a court. It is simply the name given to the totality of divine authority.

Each god is represented in the night sky by a constellation or star sign. Mortals chart the heavens not merely for navigation — but devotion.

The Universality of the Truth
The Truth is the dominant global religion.

It asserts:

The Archaeon exists.


Mithrile permeates creation.


Divine authority governs cosmic balance.


Other religions, sects, or cultural interpretations exist — often shaped by charismatic Stonehearts who claimed divinity.

But within orthodox doctrine:

The Truth is factual.
All other interpretations are incomplete or false.

Some cultures reject the gods entirely, attributing Mithrile to natural law rather than divine order.
